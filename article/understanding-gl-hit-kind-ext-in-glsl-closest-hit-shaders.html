Understanding gl_HitKindEXT in GLSL Closest-Hit Shaders

This article explores the purpose and data provided by the gl_HitKindEXT built-in variable in GLSL closest-hit shaders under the GL_EXT_ray_tracing extension. It covers how this variable communicates geometry orientation for hardware-accelerated triangles and how it transfers custom classification data from procedural intersection shaders directly to closest-hit evaluation.

What is gl_HitKindEXT?

In GLSL ray tracing pipelines, gl_HitKindEXT is a built-in input variable of type uint. It is accessible within both closest-hit shaders and any-hit shaders. The variable identifies the specific nature of the hit that caused the shader to execute, distinguishing between front-facing triangles, back-facing triangles, and custom procedural geometry hits.

Hit Kinds for Built-in Triangle Geometries

When a ray intersects standard hardware-accelerated triangle geometry, gl_HitKindEXT automatically reflects the winding orientation of the intersected primitive relative to the incoming ray. It provides one of two standardized constants:

  • gl_HitKindFrontFacingTriangleEXT (value 0xFE / 254): Indicates that the ray intersected the front face of the triangle, determined by the vertex winding order defined in the acceleration structure.
  • gl_HitKindBackFacingTriangleEXT (value 0xFF / 255): Indicates that the ray intersected the back face of the triangle.

This allows closest-hit shaders to quickly determine surface orientation without needing to perform manual dot-product comparisons between the geometric normal and the ray direction (gl_WorldRayDirectionEXT).

Hit Kinds for Custom Procedural Geometries

For procedural geometry defined by Axis-Aligned Bounding Boxes (AABBs), ray-primitive intersections are calculated manually inside an intersection shader using the reportIntersectionEXT function:

bool reportIntersectionEXT(float hitT, uint hitKind);

When an intersection is accepted, the integer value passed into the hitKind parameter is directly forwarded to the closest-hit shader and made readable through gl_HitKindEXT.

Key characteristics for procedural hit kinds include:

  • Valid Range: Custom hit kinds must be within the range of 0 to 127 (0x00 to 0x7F). Values from 128 to 255 are reserved for built-in implementation types.
  • Application-Specific Data: Developers can use this 7-bit integer to encode contextual data, such as sub-primitive types (e.g., distinguishing between the body and caps of a procedural cylinder), material IDs, or specific intersection surfaces within a complex procedural volume.

Summary of Usage in Closest-Hit Shaders

Inside the closest-hit shader, gl_HitKindEXT delivers:

  1. Facing Information: Immediate identification of front-facing versus back-facing triangle hits to flip shading normals or adjust lighting models accordingly.
  2. Intersection Metadata: User-defined identification flags emitted by custom intersection shaders, eliminating the need to pass auxiliary classification data through hit attributes or payload structures.
